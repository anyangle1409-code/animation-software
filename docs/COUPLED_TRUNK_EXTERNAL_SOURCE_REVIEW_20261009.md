# Coupled trunk — additional independently accessible source review (9 October 2026)

**STATUS: EXTERNAL SOURCES REVIEWED / NO COORDINATE TARGET SELECTED.**

This supplements (not replaces) the committed Claude/Work spinal and pelvic evidence. The project must NOT treat a source's study-average morphology as an exact 1.82-m male local-frame target, nor infer articular centres from landmarks with differing endpoints.

### A. Vertebral wedging: useful independent corroboration, not a full T1–T12 target

**Matsumoto et al., 2011**, *Wedging of vertebral bodies at the thoracolumbar junction in asymptomatic healthy subjects on magnetic resonance imaging*, Surg Radiol Anat, DOI **10.1007/s00276-010-0746-x**, PMID **21104252**.
- 115 asymptomatic individuals, 68 males and 47 females; reported anterior:posterior height ratio (APR) from MRI at T10–L2.
- APR pooled means and SD: T10 **0.92 ± 0.08**, T11 **0.92 ± 0.08**, T12 **0.90 ± 0.06**, L1 **0.89 ± 0.06**, L2 **0.90 ± 0.07**.
- Useful independent indication of wedge-shaped morphology near thoracolumbar transition, which P003's straight line segments do not model as genuine endplates.
- **Not** a per-level thoracic wedge-angle table. A/P height *ratios* alone do NOT define angle without source-matched vertebral anteroposterior depth, plane, landmarks and cohort fit.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21104252/

**Crawford et al., 2009**, *Defining normal vertebral angulation at the thoracolumbar junction*, AJR, DOI **10.2214/AJR.08.2026**, PMID **19542380**.
- Examined 200 CT lateral scouts in healthy-age 25/35-year cohorts plus 22 cadaveric vertebrae; reported endplate-angle normal means **T12 4.34°**, **L1 4.48°**; approximate reported 2-SD ranges **T12 –0.16° to 8.84°**, **L1 0.22° to 8.74°**.
- Useful **independent local endplate-angle sanity corridor** for T12–L1, not a standing sagittal tilt of the vertebra or a full thoracic slope profile. Must check study angle sign conventions, sagittal reference, specimen inclusion and whether CT projection vs bone-space coordinate measurements agree.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19542380/

**Already present in project evidence:** the 2011 human thoracic body/disc morphometry study at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3171774/ (the source used in the project's previous A-scale body-height comparison). Its per-level anterior/posterior heights can constrain shape as **a cross-check only**. Previous height analysis found its absolute T1–T12 length incompatible with the project's 1.82-m male target; do not silently reuse it to set body heights or infer wedge angles.

### B. Sternal height versus vertebral level: a distinct clinical observable

**Razzouk et al., 2023**, *Surface anatomical landmarks for spine surgery: A CT-based study of the sternal notch and sternal angle in 1,035 patients*, J Clin Neurosci, DOI **10.1016/j.jocn.2023.08.024**, PMID **37866208**.
- CT analysis: 1,035 patients, 567 males and 468 females; sternal notch vertebral levels cluster **T2–T3**, sternal angle **T4–T5**.
- Did not find a significant association of notch or sternal-angle **level** with patient height or sex.
- **Does not directly supply the AP millimetre distance from sternal notch to vertebral body** in a standing 1.82-m male. It therefore cannot close P002's 40–56 mm vs c004's 91 mm sagittal depth conflict. Landmark is notch *level*, not sternum-to-spine *distance*.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37866208/

### C. Pelvis/S1 position: frame mapping is essential

**Imai et al., 2019**, *Evaluation of anatomical pelvic parameters between normal, healthy men and women using three-dimensional computed tomography*, J Orthop Surg Res, DOI **10.1186/s13018-019-1165-2**.
- 108 healthy Japanese adults, 55 men and 53 women; male mean height around **166 cm**, below the project reference. 3D pelvis anatomically registered to the **anterior pelvic plane** (APP).
- Defined S1 endplate centre as midpoint of right/left and front/back S1 plate; measured pelvic incidence, anatomical pelvic tilt and sacral slope relative to APP.
- Confirms that **anatomical** sacral endplate coordinates are not interchangeable with **standing** pelvic-tilt pose, nor does an angle alone settle S1 AP depth in the Home Gym PT world frame.
- Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13018-019-1165-2

**Roussouly et al., 2005**, *Classification of the normal variation in the sagittal alignment of the human lumbar spine and pelvis in the standing position*, Spine, DOI **10.1097/01.brs.0000152379.54463.65**.
- Standing radiographs of **160 volunteers** showed co-variation of sacral slope and lumbar lordosis, supporting a constrained **coupled** sagittal posture instead of a uniform vertical trunk translation.
- This is an anatomical relationship, not an independent numeric S1 AP coordinate for the project without stature, pelvic incidence and endplate centre mapping.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15682018/

### Unresolved blockers after review

1. Independently sourced, endpoint-compatible **T1–T12 per-level disc/endplate sagittal inclinations** (not merely aggregate Cobb or anterior/posterior height ratios).
2. 1.82-m-male compatible **sternum-to-thoracic-vertebral-body AP centre-to-surface distance** in the same coordinate frame, not just level correspondence.
3. 3D anatomical **S1 endplate AP centre** and APP/standing/pelvis registration consistent with canonical joint-centre and hip anchors.
4. Costovertebral, costotransverse, costosternal and sternoclavicular **surface contact** geometry and moving joint frames.
5. Final solution must remain compatible with both ANSUR living-landmark stature regressions and independently sourced bone-level coordinates; never infer one from the other without a measured mapping.

**Result:** These sources narrow interpretation, but do not close the frozen canonical geometry gates or validate the P004 heuristic sternum shift. Continue read-only diagnostics, preserve a003/c001–c004 and P001/P003, and do not promote c005.
